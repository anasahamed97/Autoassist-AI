## SECTION 1: SUSPENSION & STEERING SYSTEM FUNDAMENTALS

The suspension and steering systems maintain tire contact with the road surface, absorb road shocks, isolate vehicle occupants, and provide directional control.

### Major Components:
1. MacPherson Struts / Double Wishbone: Control vertical wheel motion; incorporate coil springs, hydraulic dampers (shocks), and strut mount bearings.
2. Ball Joints: Spherical bearings providing pivotal attachment between steering knuckles and control arms.
3. Tie Rod Ends (Inner & Outer): Link the steering rack or drag link to the steering knuckle to direct wheel angle.
4. Sway Bar (Anti-Roll Bar) & End Links: Reduces body roll during cornering by transferring upward movement of one wheel to the opposite side.
5. Control Arm Bushings: Elastomeric rubber or polyurethane bushings isolating vibration and positioning the wheel assembly longitudinally and laterally.
6. Electric Power Steering (EPS) / Hydraulic Power Steering (HPS): Provides steering assist via an electric motor on the steering rack/column or an engine-driven hydraulic pump.

## SECTION 2: SYMPTOM-BASED SUSPENSION & STEERING DIAGNOSTICS

### 1. Clunking or Rattling Noise Over Bumps
- Primary Culprits:
  - Sway bar end links (worn miniature ball joints loose inside socket).
  - Front strut top mount bearings (collapsed rubber or dry needle bearings).
  - Lower control arm compliance bushings (torn rubber allowing metal-on-metal contact).
  - Worn ball joints with excessive axial or radial play.
- Diagnostic Check:
  1. Lift vehicle by frame with suspension hanging. Use a 24-inch pry bar under the tire to pry upwards; watch for vertical play in lower ball joint (>0.050 inch or 1.25 mm indicates replacement).
  2. Grasp sway bar link with channel-lock pliers and check for play.

### 2. Vehicle Pulling to One Side on Level Road
- Primary Culprits:
  - Unequal tire pressure (a 4–6 PSI difference pulls towards the lower tire).
  - Uneven camber or caster angles (vehicle pulls toward the side with more positive camber or less positive caster).
  - Sticking front brake caliper (dragging pad creates continuous braking torque on one side).
  - Radial tire pull (manufacturing belt shift within tire carcass).
- Diagnostic Check:
  1. Check and equalize all cold tire pressures to door placard specification.
  2. Swap front tires left-to-right: if the pull reverses direction, it is a radial tire pull. If the pull persists in the same direction, inspect wheel alignment or brake caliper drag.

### 3. Wheel Bearing Humming / Roaring Noise
- Symptoms: Deep humming, growling, or roaring noise that gets louder with vehicle speed; pitch changes when gently swerving the car left and right at 40 MPH.
- Rule of Thumb: Swerving right shifts vehicle weight to the left wheel bearing. If noise increases when turning right, the left wheel bearing is defective; if noise increases when turning left, the right wheel bearing is defective.
- Diagnostic Check:
  1. Raise vehicle on lift. Spin wheel by hand while resting one hand on the coil spring; roughness or vibration felt through the spring indicates pitted bearing races.

### 4. Excessive Steering Wheel Play / Wandering
- Symptoms: Steering wheel turns more than 1 inch before road wheels react; vehicle wanders on straight highway and requires constant steering correction.
- Causes:
  - Loose outer or inner tie rod ends.
  - Worn steering rack guide or intermediate steering shaft universal joint.
  - Excessive toe-out alignment angle.

## SECTION 3: WHEEL ALIGNMENT SPECIFICATIONS & TIRE WEAR PATTERNS

### Primary Alignment Angles:
1. Camber: Inward or outward tilt of wheel top viewed from front:
   - Excessive Negative Camber: Causes accelerated wear on the inside shoulder of the tire tread.
   - Excessive Positive Camber: Causes accelerated wear on the outside shoulder.
2. Toe (Toe-in / Toe-out): Parallel alignment of wheels viewed from above:
   - Toe is the primary tire-wearing angle. Even 1/8 inch (3 mm) of incorrect toe scrubs tires sideways by 28 feet per mile!
   - Feathered tread edges indicate incorrect toe setting.
3. Caster: Forward or rearward tilt of the steering axis viewed from the side:
   - Does not affect tire wear directly, but provides straight-line stability and steering returnability.
