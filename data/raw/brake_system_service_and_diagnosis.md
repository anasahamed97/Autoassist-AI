## SECTION 1: ABNORMAL BRAKE NOISE IDENTIFICATION & TROUBLESHOOTING

Brake noise is one of the most common automotive customer complaints. Different acoustic characteristics indicate specific mechanical defects.

### 1. High-Pitched Brake Squeal / Squeak:
- Acoustic Wear Sensor: Modern brake pads incorporate a small spring-steel indicator clip. When pad friction material wears down to approximately 2.0mm to 3.0mm, the clip scrapes against the brake rotor face during brake pedal application, creating an intentional loud, high-pitched screech to alert the driver before metal backing plates contact the rotor.
- High-Frequency Harmonic Vibration: Microscopic vibration between pad backing plate and caliper piston or caliper bracket abutment channels. Remedied by installing stainless steel anti-rattle hardware clips and applying high-temperature silicone brake lubricant (ceramic or synthetic) to the metal-to-metal contact points behind the pad shims (never on the friction lining).
- Glazed Brake Pads or Rotors: Hard, continuous braking or riding the brakes causes extreme friction surface overheating (> 600°C), converting resin binders into a slick, mirror-like glaze that slips and squeals against the rotor.

### 2. Harsh Metal-on-Metal Grinding Noise:
- Friction Lining Depleted: Occurs when the friction material has worn away completely. The steel pad backing plate makes direct contact with the cast-iron brake rotor. This creates severe gouging of the rotor disc surface, drastically reduces braking friction coefficient, extends stopping distances, and requires immediate replacement of both brake pads and brake rotors.
- Foreign Object Intrusion: A small stone, gravel, or road pebble lodged between the rotor surface and the sheet-metal dust shield (splash guard), creating a continuous scraping or metallic screeching noise even when the vehicle is rolling without applying brakes.

### 3. Deep Thumping / Clunking Noise:
- Loose Caliper Mounting Bolts: Caliper shifts and hits the steering knuckle when brakes are first applied in forward or reverse.
- Sticking Caliper Slide Pins: One guide pin frozen with dried grease or rust causes the caliper to cock sideways, resulting in uneven tapered pad wear and clunking upon brake release.

## SECTION 2: BRAKE VIBRATION, PULSATION, AND PEDAL FEEL DEFECTS

### Brake Pedal Pulsation / Steering Wheel Shudder:
- Rotor Thickness Variation (RTV): The primary physical cause of brake pedal pulsation. RTV exists when the brake disc rotor does not have uniform thickness across its 360-degree circumference (exceeding 0.0005 inches or 0.012 mm difference). When the thinner and thicker sections pass between the brake pads, hydraulic fluid is forced back through the caliper pistons into the master cylinder, creating a rhythmic surging in the driver's brake pedal.
- Excessive Lateral Runout: Caused by improper wheel lug nut torque sequence (using impact gun without torque wrench), rust/corrosion between wheel hub face and rotor hat, or warped rotor disc. Runout must be under 0.002 inches (0.050 mm) measured with a dial indicator.

### Spongy or Soft Brake Pedal:
- Air Entrapment in Hydraulic Lines: Air is compressible whereas hydraulic brake fluid is incompressible. When the pedal is depressed, air bubbles compress instead of pushing caliper pistons, causing a low, spongy pedal that may require "pumping" to stop. Requires pressure bleeding or two-person bleeding until fluid stream is free of bubbles.
- Moisture Contamination: DOT 3 and DOT 4 glycol-based brake fluids are hygroscopic (they absorb atmospheric moisture over time). When water content exceeds 3%, fluid boiling point drops from ~401°F (205°C) to below 284°F (140°C). During hard downhill braking, trapped water boils into steam vapor pockets, causing sudden pedal fade to the floor.
- Leaking Master Cylinder Internal Cup Seals: Internal hydraulic bypass where brake fluid slips past the primary piston rubber cup without external leakage, causing the pedal to slowly sink to the floorboard at red stoplights.
