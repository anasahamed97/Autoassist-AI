## SECTION 1: TRANSMISSION & DRIVETRAIN OVERVIEW

The drivetrain transfers rotational power from the internal combustion engine (or electric motor) through the transmission, differential, drive axles, and to the wheels.

### Core Subsystems:
1. Hydraulic Automatic Transmission (AT): Utilizes planetary gearsets, hydraulic clutches, brake bands, a valve body with pulse-width modulated (PWM) solenoids, and a hydrodynamic torque converter with a lock-up clutch.
2. Continuously Variable Transmission (CVT): Uses two variable-diameter pulleys connected by a high-strength segmented steel belt or chain to deliver infinitely variable gear ratios.
3. Manual Transmission & Clutch: Employs a dry friction clutch disc, pressure plate, release bearing (throwout bearing), and synchromesh gears.
4. Drive Axles & Constant Velocity (CV) Joints: Transfers power to steering wheels while allowing suspension travel and steering articulation. Inner CV joints are plunge joints (tripod type); outer joints are fixed ball-type (Rzeppa).
5. Differential & Final Drive: Compensates for rotational speed differences between inner and outer wheels during cornering.

## SECTION 2: TRANSMISSION FLUID ANALYSIS & CONDITION CHART

Automatic Transmission Fluid (ATF) and CVT fluid are the lifeblood of the hydraulic system, providing hydraulic pressure, lubrication, cooling, and frictional characteristics for clutch plates.

### Fluid Condition Matrix:
- Cherry Red / Translucent Pink: Fresh, healthy fluid. Normal operating condition.
- Light Brown / Transparent: Mild thermal aging. Service interval approaching within 10,000 to 15,000 miles.
- Dark Brown / Opaque with Sweet/Burnt Acrid Odor: Severe fluid oxidation. Friction plate glaze and clutch lining breakdown. Fluid flush and pan drop filter replacement required immediately.
- Pitch Black with Metallic Flakes or Silt: Severe mechanical failure. Clutch pack friction material disintegrated or planetary gear scoring. Transmission overhaul or replacement necessary; simple fluid flush may cause slipping.
- Milky Pink / Strawberry Milkshake Appearance: Catastrophic contamination. Engine coolant has breached the transmission oil cooler integrated into the radiator lower tank. Water degrades friction linings and swells seals. Requires cooling system repair and complete transmission overhaul.

## SECTION 3: COMMON TRANSMISSION SYMPTOMS & DIAGNOSTIC PROCEDURES

### 1. Transmission Slipping (Engine RPM Rises Without Acceleration)
- Symptoms: Engine revs flair between shifts; vehicle hesitates or feels like it entered neutral when accelerating.
- Causes:
  - Low ATF level (causes pump cavitation and hydraulic pressure drop).
  - Worn friction clutch pack plates or burned clutch friction material.
  - Defective shift solenoid valve or stuck pressure regulator spool in the valve body.
  - Internal piston seal leakage.
- Diagnostic Action:
  1. Check fluid level on level ground with engine at operating temperature and idling in Park or Neutral.
  2. Perform line pressure test using a 0–300 PSI hydraulic pressure gauge connected to the transmission main line pressure tap.
  3. Scan for DTC codes: P0730 (Incorrect Gear Ratio), P0731 (Gear 1 Incorrect Ratio), P0732, P0733, P0734, P0735.

### 2. Harsh Shift Engagement or Clunk When Shifting to Drive/Reverse
- Symptoms: Hard jolt when shifting from Park to Drive or Reverse; banging between 1st and 2nd gear.
- Causes:
  - Broken or deteriorated engine/transmission mounts (rubber bushing collapsed).
  - High line pressure caused by malfunctioning EPC (Electronic Pressure Control) solenoid.
  - Defective Throttle Position Sensor (TPS) or MAP sensor reporting inaccurate engine load to TCM.
  - U-joint or CV axle excessive backlash.
- Diagnostic Action:
  1. Power-brake test with helper observing engine roll: excessive engine lift (>1.5 inches) indicates broken mount.
  2. Inspect CV axle inner and outer joints for rotational play.

### 3. Torque Converter Shudder (Vibration at 35–50 MPH)
- Symptoms: Chattering or rumble strip sensation during light acceleration between 35 and 50 MPH when the torque converter lockup clutch engages.
- Causes:
  - Degraded friction modifiers in ATF fluid.
  - Worn torque converter lockup clutch friction disc.
  - Faulty TCC (Torque Converter Clutch) PWM solenoid.
- Diagnostic Action:
  1. Lightly tap brake pedal with left foot while shudder occurs (without slowing down). If shudder stops immediately, the TCM just disengaged the lockup clutch, confirming TCC shudder.
  2. Perform complete fluid exchange with manufacturer-specified OEM synthetic fluid.

### 4. Constant Velocity (CV) Axle Clicking & Clunking
- Symptoms: Rapid rhythmic clicking or popping noise when accelerating while turning sharp corners (e.g., pulling into a parking spot).
- Causes: Torn CV boot allowing grease to escape and road grit to enter, causing excessive play and galling in the outer Rzeppa ball bearings.
- Diagnostic Action:
  1. Inspect outer rubber boots for tears, grease splatter on brake caliper and inner wheel rim.
  2. Replace half-shaft assembly if boots are torn and joint is clicking.
