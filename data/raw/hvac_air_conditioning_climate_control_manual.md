## SECTION 1: REFRIGERATION CYCLE & OPERATING PRINCIPLES

Automotive air conditioning operates via a closed vapor-compression refrigeration cycle using R-134a or R-1234yf refrigerant and polyalkylene glycol (PAG) lubricant.

### Major System Components:
1. A/C Compressor: Driven by serpentine belt with electromagnetic clutch or variable-displacement pulse-modulated control valve. Compresses low-pressure vapor into high-pressure superheated vapor.
2. Condenser: Heat exchanger mounted in front of the engine radiator. Dissipates heat to airflow, condensing high-pressure vapor into high-pressure liquid.
3. Receiver-Drier / Accumulator: Contains desiccant to absorb moisture and a filter to trap contaminants.
4. Thermal Expansion Valve (TXV) / Orifice Tube: Meters liquid refrigerant into evaporator, creating a pressure drop that causes refrigerant to boil and absorb cabin heat.
5. Evaporator Core: Located inside the cabin HVAC heater box. Cools and dehumidifies cabin air blown across its fins.
6. Blend Door Actuators: Miniature electric stepper motors that position doors inside the HVAC box to mix hot air (heater core) and cold air (evaporator core).

## SECTION 2: SYSTEM PRESSURE DIAGNOSTICS WITH MANIFOLD GAUGES

Operating pressures measured at 1,500 Engine RPM, ambient temperature 85°F (29°C), with A/C set to MAX Cold and blower on HIGH:
- Normal R-134a Operating Pressures:
  - Low Side: 25 to 35 PSI (1.7 to 2.4 bar)
  - High Side: 150 to 200 PSI (10.3 to 13.8 bar)
  - Center Vent Output: 38°F to 48°F (3°C to 9°C)

### Common Diagnostic Pressure Scenarios:
1. Low Side Very Low (<15 PSI), High Side Low (<100 PSI):
   - Condition: Low refrigerant charge caused by a leak.
   - Diagnostic: Check for UV dye traces around compressor shaft seal, condenser rock hits, and service port Schrader valves.
2. Low Side High (>50 PSI), High Side High (>300 PSI):
   - Condition: Inadequate condenser cooling or overcharged refrigerant.
   - Diagnostic: Inspect condenser cooling fan operation; clean bugs/debris from condenser fins.
3. Low Side High (>50 PSI), High Side Low (<120 PSI):
   - Condition: Failed compressor internal reeds/pistons (compressor cannot pump pressure differential).
   - Diagnostic: Compressor replacement, system flush, and new receiver-drier required.
4. Low Side Pulls into Vacuum (<0 PSI), High Side Normal to High:
   - Condition: Restricted expansion valve (TXV) or moisture frozen inside orifice tube.

## SECTION 3: HVAC CABIN CLIMATE CONTROL SYMPTOMS

### 1. Rapid Clicking Noise Behind Dashboard When Turning Key On
- Cause: Stripped nylon gear inside the HVAC blend door actuator or mode door actuator. When the climate computer initializes, the motor spins against broken teeth.
- Repair: Replace the specific blend door actuator (driver side, passenger side, or recirculation door).

### 2. Musty / Vinegar / Locker-Room Odor from A/C Vents
- Cause: Mold, mildew, and bacterial growth on wet evaporator core fins and inside the HVAC condensate drain tray.
- Repair: Verify evaporator drain tube is dripping water under vehicle (unclog with compressed air if blocked); apply foaming evaporator cleaner disinfectant directly through drain hose or blower motor opening; replace cabin air filter.

### 3. A/C Blows Cold at Highway Speeds But Warm at Idle or in Traffic
- Cause: Auxiliary electric condenser cooling fan motor inoperative, blown fan fuse, or weak fan relay. At highway speeds, vehicle ram air cools the condenser, but at idle, heat builds up, high-side pressure triggers the high-pressure safety cutout switch, and the compressor disengages.
